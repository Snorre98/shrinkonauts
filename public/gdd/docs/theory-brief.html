Purpose: the source for the GBL theory section (≤1000 words). It selects the lenses, maps each to a mechanic and evidence, maps the six course readings, and defines claims/non-claims. The theory section applies this brief; it does not explain the theories.

1. Selection

Four core lenses + one supporting lens:

Malone Intrinsic fantasy, curiosity, control, challenge The body/system is the level; exploration and gates are the motivation
Self-determination theory (SDT) Autonomy, competence, relatedness Route choice, ability mastery, co-op/agent relationships
Cognitive load Segmenting, signalling, dual coding, spatial contiguity One concept per level; scan/HUD; dialogue + visual + map
Constructive alignment Objectives → mechanics → evidence The syllabus/mapping/evidence matrix

3. Course readings: use/cut

The GBL syllabus readings are the six IT3021 texts. Use them selectively; the theory section should not become a literature review.

Reading Use Cut/qualify Why
Prensky 2003 Learning through experimentation, complex systems, fun “Digital native” generalisations Supports exploration/curiosity; the game is experimentation, not a lecture
Kiili 2005 Experiential gaming model: experience, reflection, flow Detailed cognitive-load theory Supports the loop/debrief; keep as supporting lens
Wang & Lieberoth 2016 Points/audio can affect engagement/concentration The specific Kahoot experiment The game has no points; cite as a design trade-off, not a claim
Wang & Tahir 2020 Classroom dynamics, anxiety, competition risks Kahoot-specific findings Supports no leaderboard/no punitive scoring
Wang et al. 2024 GameFlow/Malone, embedded learning, feedback, social interaction Project-management context Supports the loop/co-op/feedback
Tahir & Wang 2018 (LEAGUE) Evaluation dimensions Full LEAGUE log Use only if the appendix is submitted; otherwise cite as evaluation framing

Rule: at least 3–4 of the six readings should appear in the theory section; not all six. Each citation must support a design choice, not decorate the text.

4. Claims and non-claims

Claims the design can support:

  • The game supports explanation and prediction of the knee-jerk reflex arc in a short session.
  • The fantasy is intrinsic: the body/system is the level and anatomy knowledge is the key.
  • Concept gates and the debrief make understanding the progression mechanism.
  • Co-op supports peer explanation (relatedness/teach-back), but is not required.
  • The map/concept log externalise the mental model.

Non-claims:

  • No exam-score improvement.
  • No full Biologi 1/2 curriculum coverage.
  • No clinical/medical training.
  • No large-scale efficacy study.
  • No claim that points/leaderboards are necessary or harmful in general.
  • No claim that the game replaces a teacher, lab, or textbook.

5. Cross-report consistency

Issue Decision
Intrinsic vs extrinsic fantasy Kjøpesmart’s cozy home is extrinsic (a reward layer around shopping); Shrinkonauts’ body is intrinsic (the system is the level; knowledge is the key). The GBL theory must say this explicitly.
Game vs gamification This is a game-based learning application, not a gamified app. Do not import Octalysis/player types from the Gamification report unless directly relevant.
ExerGame (REF!) Running is intrinsic to the referee role; that is different from Kjøpesmart’s extrinsic home. The GBL anatomy fantasy is intrinsic. Keep the distinctions consistent if E-T6 is used.
Theory names Use the same lens names/features in the GDD Learning section, the theory section, and the video.
Evidence IDs Use OBJ-1..5, CON-1..8, EV-1..6; no new IDs without updating the syllabus/mapping.

6. Evaluation plan

  • Participants: 3 external players/pairs (VG2 biology if possible; otherwise 16–19 with basic biology).
  • Pre/post: 4 short questions — identify, trace, explain, predict.
  • Think-aloud: observe gate attempts, hints, confusion, co-op talk.
  • Debrief: free-text answer; 0–2 rubric (0 labels only, 1 partial bypass, 2 correct bypass + brain later).
  • Game evidence: export debrief_answered, gate_attempted/solved/failed, hint_used.
  • Analysis: descriptive; compare pre/post at the individual level; quote the debrief answers; no inferential statistics.
  • Claim: formative evidence of explanation/prediction; not efficacy.

9. What the theory section must not do

  • Explain Malone/SDT/cognitive load/constructive alignment in general.
  • Cite all six readings without using them.
  • Claim efficacy from a 3-person playtest.
  • Use “gamification” language (points, badges, leaderboards) unless the game actually uses it.
  • Claim VG3/full-curriculum alignment.
  • Contradict the GDD/video features.
