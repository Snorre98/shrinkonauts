ADR-0075: Hero-asset treatment profiles and per-class fit pipeline

Copied from the working docs. Some cross-links may still point to the original repository or Obsidian vault.

  • Status: Proposed
  • Date: 2026-11-02
  • Deciders: Snorre
  • Related: ADR-0047, ADR-0048, ADR-0060, ADR-0061, ADR-0062, ADR-0063, ADR-0065, ADR-0072, ADR-0074
  • Extends: ADR-0060–0065 (source/work/runtime layers, palette LUTs, animation, previews) and ADR-0072 §3–5 (fidelity classes, readability gates, and encoding).
  • Supersedes: the single global cell in the fit configuration, the fixed palette-cell match, and the 18×18 entity / 24×24 prop target rows in ADR-0072.

Context

The current fit pipeline assumes one cell for every sprite:

  • scripts/art-fit.ts reads one top-level cell, one margin, and one set of gates for every asset.
  • scripts/art/palette.ts and scripts/art-build-player.ts require the palette's cell to match the fit cell.
  • fit.new.config.json and fit.silicon.config.json are both 18×18.
  • The Neuron Keeper source is 267×264 per frame; 18×18 is a 14.8:1 downscale. The concept plates are 2048×2048 or larger; the current fit is often 90–120:1 per axis.

ADR-0074 introduces three hero roles with 1:1 display: npc at 96×96, interactable at 64×64, and prop at 48×48. Those sizes cannot share a single 18×18 cell or a single palette cell. The pipeline also needs a treatment record: a mathematical fit is not the same as a hand-cleaned hero asset, and a 2048→96 blind squeeze is not an acceptable final.

The runtime stays lossless. "Less compressed" here means less source-to-cell downscaling, not lower WebP quality.

Decision

1. Classes and gates

Add per-class cells and gates. A config declares a classes map; each asset selects one class and may override cell, display, or treatment.

Class Cell Display minReadablePx minOpaquePixels maxPaletteDistance maxDownscale
hero-npc 96×96 96×96 72 3500 24 4:1
hero-interactable 64×64 64×64 48 1400 24 10:1
hero-prop 48×48 48×48 32 600 24 12:1
ui-1x scan 36×36 36×36 24 300 24 10:1
world-2x player 14×22 28×44 14 300 32 8:1
world-2x decor 18×18 36×36 8 100 32 16:1

maxDownscale is the ratio between the cropped source content bounds and the fitted content, measured per axis. The Keeper at 267→96 is 2.8:1 and passes. A 2048×2048 CPU plate with a 1200 px subject at 96 px is 12.5:1 and fails; that is intentional. The asset must be cropped, redrawn, or hand-cleaned rather than mechanically squeezed.

The gates are warnings for fit-preview and hard gates for hand-cleaned final assets. A recorded exception is required to ship a final asset outside a gate.

2. Treatment profiles

The fit tool records a treatment and the artist works to it:

  • hero-npc — silhouette-first; 1 px ink outline; minimum 2×2 colour clusters at display size; one warm nerve accent (lantern/nucleus) and one joint-cyan accent; hand-cleaned; stable baseline across frames; static frame retained for reduced motion.
  • hero-interactable — 1 px outline; a joint-cyan functional accent on the edge the player uses; hand-cleaned; readable at 64 px; no interior detail smaller than 2×2 display pixels.
  • hero-prop — 1 px outline; accent only when carried or active; hand-cleaned; readable at 48 px.
  • ui-1x scan — existing diagram treatment; high contrast on #101a20; 2×2 clusters; no world-scale upscale.
  • world-2x player — unchanged; 14×22 master, 28×44 display.
  • world-2x decor — unchanged; 18×18 master, 36×36 display.

A hero asset may use the existing three-step ramps. Detail comes from silhouette, dither, outline, and accent placement, not from adding colours. No new off-palette colours and no anti-aliasing.

3. Configuration

The fit config moves to schema version 2:

{
  "schemaVersion": 2,
  "palette": "art-source/nanobanan/palette.json",
  "classes": {
    "hero-npc": {
      "cell": [96, 96],
      "display": [96, 96],
      "margin": 2,
      "minReadablePx": 72,
      "minOpaquePixels": 3500,
      "maxPaletteDistance": 24,
      "maxDownscale": 4,
      "treatment": "hero-npc"
    },
    "hero-interactable": {
      "cell": [64, 64],
      "display": [64, 64],
      "margin": 2,
      "minReadablePx": 48,
      "minOpaquePixels": 1400,
      "maxPaletteDistance": 24,
      "maxDownscale": 10,
      "treatment": "hero-interactable"
    }
  },
  "assets": [
    {
      "id": "sprite.agent.neuron",
      "class": "hero-npc",
      "status": "fit-preview",
      "source": "art-source/nanobanan/keyed/agent-neuron.png",
      "output": "public/packs/anatomy-reflex/shared/sprites/agent-neuron.png",
      "runtime": true,
      "ramps": ["teal", "bone", "boneShadow", "nerve", "joint", "sage", "ink"]
    }
  ]
}

Rules:

  1. classes is required in schema 2. A per-asset cell, display, margin, minReadablePx, minOpaquePixels, maxPaletteDistance, maxDownscale, or treatment overrides the class value.
  2. The palette document's cell becomes optional. When present it is advisory; the fit cell wins. This is required because P1/P2, the Keeper, and hero objects use different cells.
  3. schemaVersion: 1 configs remain readable and keep the current 18×18 behaviour until migrated.
  4. The report records class, cell, display, fidelity, treatment, sourceScale, the gates, and the status for every asset.

4. Previews and contact sheets

The current 2×/8× preview is not enough to judge the hierarchy:

  • 1× display preview: the actual runtime size.
  • 2× display preview: pixel inspection.
  • 4× inspection preview: cleanup review.
  • Stage-scale preview: crop at 1280×504, camera zoom 2, with a 28×44 player silhouette and the actual background swatch behind the asset.
  • Pack contact sheet: every role side by side with the player silhouette.

The stage-scale preview is the acceptance surface for "astronauts look small" and "objects stand out". It is generated from the runtime export, not from the untouched source plate.

5. Animation

art-source/characters/neuron-agent/working/clips.json moves to a 96×96 cell and a 96×96 display. The existing 36-frame idle loop becomes a 3456×96 sheet.

  • The static frame remains frame 0.
  • The idle loop remains 0–35 at 12.5 fps.
  • Reduced motion keeps the static frame.
  • Hero animation previews use 2× and 4×, not the player's 8×; the sheet GIF is not rendered at 8×.
  • sprite.agent.cpu ships as a 96×96 static until an idle sheet exists.

6. Encoding and budgets

The encoding policy from ADR-0072 is unchanged:

  • Pixel art: PNG or lossless WebP only.
  • No JPEG, no lossy WebP, no palette quantisation after hand cleanup.
  • Alpha is hard 0 or 255; no magenta fringe; no anti-aliasing.

Starting byte budgets, to be rebaselined after the first export:

Class Budget
hero-npc static ≤ 32 KiB
hero-npc idle sheet (36 frames) ≤ 512 KiB
hero-interactable ≤ 16 KiB
hero-prop ≤ 8 KiB
ui-1x scan ≤ 16 KiB
world-2x player sheet existing budget

7. Status and gates

ADR-0072's status split remains: fit-preview, hand-cleaned, placeholder, alternate, reserve.

  • A blind fit may ship as fit-preview in a POC build.
  • Only a hand-cleaned asset may be final.
  • A hero asset that misses minReadablePx, minOpaquePixels, maxPaletteDistance, or maxDownscale is visibly marked in the report.
  • No fit-preview asset is labelled final.

8. Asset mapping

sprite.agent.neuron static + idle 18×18 / 36×36 96×96 / 96×96, hero-npc, sheet 96
sprite.agent.cpu 18×18 / 36×36 96×96 / 96×96, hero-npc
sprite.gate.ferry, sprite.gate.actuator 18×18 / 36×36 64×64 / 64×64, hero-interactable
sprite.pulse.terminal, sprite.console.*, sprite.socket.*, sprite.exit* 18×18 / 36×36 64×64 / 64×64, hero-interactable
sprite.prop.tracer, sprite.prop.route_plug 18×18 / 36×36 48×48 / 48×48, hero-prop
sprite.receptor.* 18×18 / 36×36 64×64 / 64×64, hero-interactable
sprite.scan.* 16×16 / 36×36 36×36 / 36×36, ui-1x
sprite.pad.dendrite, sprite.pad.terminal new 64×64 / 64×64, hero-interactable
sprite.pad.sensor 18×18 / 36×36 64×64 / 64×64, hero-interactable
Players 14×22 / 28×44 unchanged

The floating scan plates and the physical world pads must be separate asset IDs. Reusing a 36×36 scan plate at 64×64 would upscale it and break the 1:1 rule.

Consequences

Easier:

  • Each role gets the cell and gates that match its job.
  • The Keeper and interactables can be hand-cleaned at their real display size.
  • The report records whether an asset is a fit or a final.
  • The stage-scale contact sheet makes the size hierarchy reviewable.
  • P1/P2, decor, atlas, and encoding rules stay stable.

Harder:

  • The fit config and palette parsing need a schema migration.
  • The Keeper sheet grows from 648×18 to 3456×96; preview and byte budgets must be rechecked.
  • Every hero asset needs a hand-clean pass; a blind fit is not final.
  • The CPU plate fails the 4:1 gate on a blind fit and needs a crop/redraw.
  • Reports, contact sheets, and tests must understand classes and treatments.
  • The pipeline must keep per-asset cells from leaking into unrelated assets.

Constrained:

  • No single global cell for all assets.
  • No 1:1 upscale of a smaller source.
  • No hero asset marked final while it is fit-preview.
  • No lossy encoding or post-clean quantisation.
  • No mixing of pixel densities inside one asset.
  • No raw art-source/ file referenced by a runtime manifest.

Validation

  • npm run art:fit -- --config <config> --no-write reports the class, cell, display, source scale, gates, and status for every asset.
  • The Keeper's fit reports a source scale of approximately 2.8:1 and passes the hero-npc gates after hand cleaning.
  • A 2048×2048 CPU plate either passes the 4:1 gate after cropping or is marked fit-preview with the failure visible.
  • Every final hero asset has maxPaletteDistance ≤ 24, hard alpha, no magenta fringe, and 2×2 clusters at display size.
  • The stage-scale contact sheet shows the player at 28×44 and the NPC at 96×96 in the same 1280×504 crop.
  • npm test, npm run pack:validate, npm run art:build-player, and the 1280×720 capture review pass.
  • No JPEG or lossy WebP is present under public/.
  • A schema-1 config still runs with the legacy 18×18 behaviour.

Alternatives considered

  • Keep the global 18×18 cell — rejected: it is the direct cause of the detail loss and cannot express the hero roles.
  • Use one 96×96 cell for everything — rejected: simple props and decor would become oversized and expensive.
  • Use a single 1:1 cell and scale every asset — rejected: it breaks the world-2x actors and inflates file sizes.
  • Lower WebP quality — rejected: the runtime is lossless; quality loss does not restore detail.
  • Keep poc-integrated as the final status — rejected: it hides the difference between a mathematical fit and approved art.
  • Generate hero art only with the fit tool — rejected: a blind squeeze from a 2048 px plate will not produce a readable 96 px character.
